# Avionics EAA SportAir Workshop

About once a year, the EAA comes to Lawrenceville to provide a few classes in airplane construction.  They call these classes the <a href="http://www.sportair.com">"EAA SportAir Workshops"</a>.  I took the "Sheet Metal Basics" course last year, just before I ordered the empennage.  This year, I figured the best class to take was the "Electrical Systems &amp; Avionics" course.  The big change this year was that the classes were not being held at KLZU, rather they were at the Aviation Institute of Maintenance...  which moved from KLZU about a year or so ago.  Their new facility is actually quite nice.  While waiting for registration to begin, I took a few pictures of the various equipment and projects that were being worked on.

The avionics class itself was held in a classroom down the hall, but more suited to the material that we would be going over.  The highlights of the class were of course the hands on projects.  The first of which was attaching ends to wires.  The "crimping exercise" consisted of two 6" wires (AWG 18), a ring connector, a butt connector, and a molex connector.  The goal is perform an example of the connectors common for most power and avionics systems.

<a href="http://www.brianmichael.org/media/rv10/2011/11_06_2011/IMG_2079.jpg"><img style="margin: 5px;" src="http://www.brianmichael.org/media/rv10/2011/11_06_2011/IMG_2079_SM.jpg" alt="" width="200" height="149" /></a>

The next project was a typical headset harness.  This one required the use of soldering, shrink tubing, and being able to read a wiring diagram.  After everything was assembled, we got to see if we did it right by hooking up the harness to an actual headset (and a tester).  I was pleased to have mine work on the first try :-)

After a couple more hours of lecture, it was time for the 3rd exercise.  This time it was a BNC crimping exercise.  We were handed a piece of RG-58 and a BNC connector.  The key to this exercise was getting the lengths of each layer of the wire correct.  In the end, not all that hard to do.

After lunch, there was time for just a few more tips and tricks, and then we were off onto our last exercise.  The panel wiring exercise is designed to use most of what is learned in the entire course.  It includes a battery, power and ground bus, light bulbs for each circuit, a switch, fuse, and a dimmer.  All of the rest of the work (like building the aluminum box) was done for us.

<a href="http://www.brianmichael.org/media/rv10/2011/11_06_2011/IMG_2095.jpg"><img style="margin: 5px;" src="http://www.brianmichael.org/media/rv10/2011/11_06_2011/IMG_2095_SM.jpg" alt="" width="200" height="149" /></a><a href="http://www.brianmichael.org/media/rv10/2011/11_06_2011/IMG_2096.jpg"><img style="margin: 5px;" src="http://www.brianmichael.org/media/rv10/2011/11_06_2011/IMG_2096_SM.jpg" alt="" width="200" height="149" /></a>

All-in-all, I'd say the class is definitely worth the time and money, as I certainly learned a lot, and was made aware of some items that I might include in my plane when it comes time to add the electrical system to it.
