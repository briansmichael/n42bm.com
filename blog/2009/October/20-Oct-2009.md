<p style="margin-top: 0px; margin-right: 0px; margin-bottom: 0px; margin-left: 52px; text-indent: -52px; font: normal normal normal 12px/normal Helvetica;"><span style="font-family: Georgia, 'Times New Roman', 'Bitstream Charter', Times, serif; font-size: 13px; line-height: 19px;">4:00 AM - Wake up.  I can't sleep, too much going on in my head... most of it aviation related.  For the next few hours I'm going over all the paperwork and getting ready for the day.</span></p>
<p style="margin-top: 0px; margin-right: 0px; margin-bottom: 0px; margin-left: 52px; text-indent: -52px; font: normal normal normal 12px/normal Helvetica;"><span style="font-family: Georgia, 'Times New Roman', 'Bitstream Charter', Times, serif; font-size: 13px; line-height: 19px;">6:30 AM - At the airport.  A little early, yes, but I didn't know what Atlanta traffic would do and I wasn't going to be late for anything!  Needless to say, I sat in the car reading more about weather and the FARs.</span></p>
<p style="margin-top: 0px; margin-right: 0px; margin-bottom: 0px; margin-left: 52px; text-indent: -52px; font: normal normal normal 12px/normal Helvetica;"><span style="font-family: Georgia, 'Times New Roman', 'Bitstream Charter', Times, serif; font-size: 13px; line-height: 19px;">7:00 AM - Ernie drives up, and we begin the pre-flight of the airplane.  The temperature was 4C and the dew point was also 4C, and the plane had a thin layer of ice covering the top of the wings and the windshield.  I must have used 1/2 of the spray bottle (alcohol) de-icing the wings.  The windshield was the hard one.  I didn't know what effect the alcohol would have on the windshield, so I sprayed a light layer on it, then using a bucket of water, washed it off.  I think the only way the ice ever got off that windshield was due to the heat in my hands... boy was that cold!</span></p>

<p style="margin-top: 0px; margin-right: 0px; margin-bottom: 0px; margin-left: 52px; text-indent: -52px; font: normal normal normal 12px/normal Helvetica;"><span style="font-family: Georgia, 'Times New Roman', 'Bitstream Charter', Times, serif; font-size: 13px; line-height: 19px;">12:00 PM - Oral complete.  I was so caught up in the oral, that I really didn't pay any attention to what Ernie was doing, or anyone else for that matter.  We turned around and saw Ernie chatting with a couple military guys (I think Marines, but they may have been Navy).  He saw that we were finished and said "is it lunch time yet?"  We were all ready to get a bit to eat, and I really wanted to try out this bar-be-que place that everyone raves about.  So we head over to the bar-be-que place and each of us get the pork plate.  I must say it was quite good.</span></p>
<p style="margin-top: 0px; margin-right: 0px; margin-bottom: 0px; margin-left: 52px; text-indent: -52px; font: normal normal normal 12px/normal Helvetica;"><span style="font-family: Georgia, 'Times New Roman', 'Bitstream Charter', Times, serif; font-size: 13px; line-height: 19px;">1:30 PM - Practical test begins.  We start off with a preflight check.  He said to treat it as if it was the first flight of the day.  He really wasn't very scrutinizing on how I did it, but did ask the occasional question about what I was looking for, or what something was.  For example, the V shaped antenna on the empennage are the VOR antenna.  The rubber belt inside the cowling is the alternator belt (not the starter, as I answered.  I got a bit carried away :-) ).  With the pre-flight complete we get in the plane.  He tells me that I don't have to explain how the seat belt works (being nice) and that in case of an emergency he would let me handle it to the best of my ability, but would take over if he saw it was too much for me.  I guess a little self-preservation on his part.  I didn't mind.  In the same sense of humor, I let him know I needed to call out "clear prop" to start the engine, but no one would likely hear me, as there were a couple Navy jets behind us doing a run-up.  I couldn't hear a thing. </span></p>
<p style="margin-top: 0px; margin-right: 0px; margin-bottom: 0px; margin-left: 52px; text-indent: -52px; font: normal normal normal 12px/normal Helvetica;"><span style="font-family: Georgia, 'Times New Roman', 'Bitstream Charter', Times, serif; font-size: 13px; line-height: 19px;">Engine started, I contact Chattanooga Clearance and let them know that I'm heading to the southeast at 5,500 feet.  I'm not too good at talking to clearance delivery at this point, as I had only done it twice before, so naturally I made a few mistakes in my call.  Ben didn't seem to think that was a problem, especially since I knew I had made mistakes immediately after making them, and corrected myself where appropriate.  After I get my clearance, I contact ground and let them know I'm ready to move.  Again, making the mistake of not telling ground where I was.  They didn't ask, so I guess they kindof knew, and gave me taxi instructions to runway 20.  While taxi'ing, Ben made sure to caution me about staying away from the new red and white line around the terminal building.  Apparently they have motion sensors around the building... boy is the TSA paranoid!  I complete the run up behind a couple Cessna Citations, and make my way to the runway.</span></p>
<p style="margin-top: 0px; margin-right: 0px; margin-bottom: 0px; margin-left: 52px; text-indent: -52px; font: normal normal normal 12px/normal Helvetica;"><span style="font-family: Georgia, 'Times New Roman', 'Bitstream Charter', Times, serif; font-size: 13px; line-height: 19px;">I switch over to tower's frequency and let them know I'm ready for departure.  Ben wanted me to do a normal departure for the first one, just to get a feel for what he was in for :-).  I think I did quite well, keeping the plane in line with the runway centerline until about 700 feet AGL.  Then turned to the left as directed by my clearance, and started getting on my cross country route as I had planned a few days prior.</span></p>
<p style="margin-top: 0px; margin-right: 0px; margin-bottom: 0px; margin-left: 52px; text-indent: -52px; font: normal normal normal 12px/normal Helvetica;"><span style="font-family: Georgia, 'Times New Roman', 'Bitstream Charter', Times, serif; font-size: 13px; line-height: 19px;">Once I get on my desired heading, now it is just a matter of getting up to altitude, but Ben cuts me short and states that I did fine and to abort the cross country and divert to KDNN (Dalton, GA).  He didn't want to waste our time getting up 5,500 just to descend back down for some landings at Dalton.  He then asked me to do a short field landing at Dalton.  I tuned into the AWOS station at Dalton and noted the current weather.  Then tuned into the CTAF for Dalton and didn't hear anyone else in the pattern, so I radio'ed for airport advisories.  The UNICOM operator came up and said they were landing runway 32 and that no one else was in the pattern.  I thanked him, and started a 45 entry into downwind.  The biggest issue I had was remembering to use "Dalton" and not "Winder" in my radio calls.  Oh how we get into ruts at times :-)  Ben just laughed, and said he'd experienced the same.  I did the remainder of the calls, on down wind, turning base, and turning final.  Then came in for what I think is my best short field landing yet.  He didn't want me to brake too hard, so I didn't and we turned off at the first available taxi way.</span></p>
<p style="margin-top: 0px; margin-right: 0px; margin-bottom: 0px; margin-left: 52px; text-indent: -52px; font: normal normal normal 12px/normal Helvetica;"><span style="font-family: Georgia, 'Times New Roman', 'Bitstream Charter', Times, serif; font-size: 13px; line-height: 19px;">So far, no problems, so he had me setup for a short field take off.  I did, and we were off again.  This time I stayed in the pattern, doing my call outs as I'm supposed to, setting up for a soft field landing.  I was a little high on my approach, but still managed to land within 200 feet of my designated touch down point.  Again, turning onto the first available taxi way.  Back taxi'ing, Ben let me know the next take off was to be a soft field one.  I did as asked, and we performed a straight out departure of the pattern to the north.</span></p>
<p style="margin-top: 0px; margin-right: 0px; margin-bottom: 0px; margin-left: 52px; text-indent: -52px; font: normal normal normal 12px/normal Helvetica;"><span style="font-family: Georgia, 'Times New Roman', 'Bitstream Charter', Times, serif; font-size: 13px; line-height: 19px;">Once we were far enough away from Dalton, he had me do a turn around a point, but cautioned me to find a point with a field nearby, as I was to expect an engine failure shortly after completing the maneuver.  If only all engine failures were as predictable as that!  I had a small problem initiating the maneuver, as I had picked out a point, but lost to point while turning the plane to do a clearing turn.  After a few moments, I found the point again, and around I went.  Just as soon as I had straightened out from the turn, he said, "ok, you just had an engine failure".  At 800 feet AGL, that is a particularly bad time to experience such a failure... but the field right next to the point (a small radio tower) was really easy to line up and land in.  Once Ben was satisfied that I could make the field, we pushed back in the throttle, and climbed back to about 3,000 feet MSL.  Then it was time for a power off stall.  I do my clearing turn, and go right into the stall.  I've done these sooo many times, it was second nature to me.  With that out of the way, he had me go into a power on stall.  Also, no problems on the power on stall.</span></p>
<p style="margin-top: 0px; margin-right: 0px; margin-bottom: 0px; margin-left: 52px; text-indent: -52px; font: normal normal normal 12px/normal Helvetica;"><span style="font-family: Georgia, 'Times New Roman', 'Bitstream Charter', Times, serif; font-size: 13px; line-height: 19px;">We then contact KCHA approach and let them know we are coming back into their airspace for landing.  They are not too busy, so give us instructions to head on in to runway 20.  The landing was uneventful, and we taxi'ed back to TAC Air south.</span></p>
<p style="margin-top: 0px; margin-right: 0px; margin-bottom: 0px; margin-left: 52px; text-indent: -52px; font: normal normal normal 12px/normal Helvetica;"><span style="font-family: Georgia, 'Times New Roman', 'Bitstream Charter', Times, serif; font-size: 13px; line-height: 19px;">3:00 PM - Practical test complete.  Ben logs into the FAA system and sets me up as a newly licensed pilot!</span></p>
